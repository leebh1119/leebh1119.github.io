Why did I do that?

The Korean stock market was closed today, and I stayed up all night.

I went through the systems that I had suddenly added, since early July, analysing them one by one.

When the systems were performing well, I had nothing to worry about. But the sideways market that started in August began to eat into the returns of my Korean portfolio.

It was the sort of routine situation that comes around once every 5 or 6 months, so I tried to shrug it off. Still, something didn't feel quite right. It felt like I had a bit too much leverage on. Not excessively so, but that was just the feeling I had.

I went back and analysed the past 10 years of equity curves for each of the 20 individual systems in my Korean portfolio.

I also looked at their correlations.

Correlation is obviously something I consider when adding new systems, but there were still several systems with very high correlations. Some were above 0.8, and even above 0.9.

That was probably why the leverage had felt excessive over the past couple of weeks.

When returns were very strong, like they had been up until the end of July, I was simply happy with the results and didn't think much of it. But once the returns started to shrink, I began to feel that there was some kind of concentration or imbalance that I couldn't quite put my finger on.

Fortunately, the losses were nowhere near catastrophic. They were still well within the MDD range, so I took it as a relatively fortunate outcome and rebuilt the system portfolio without hesitation. After all, that leverage was also what helped me make a profit in July.

The five US systems are currently running without any issues, while I have reduced the Korean portfolio from 20 systems to 14. I also took this opportunity to make the leverage calculation a bit more reasonable.

The systems handle all entry and exit decisions. But deciding which systems to add to the portfolio, and ultimately which ones to remove, is the quant's job—and a discretionary decision.
