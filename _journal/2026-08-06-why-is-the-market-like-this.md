On my way home after finishing my workout earlier, I noticed a bloke who looked like one of the neighbours talking on the phone.

His voice was so loud that, even though I wasn’t trying to listen in, it was pretty much impossible not to hear him.

From what I could gather, the conversation went something like this:

“Is this really how the market is supposed to work? I just don’t get it.”

He was going on about fundamentals, how the US market was hitting all-time highs, and this and that. From the sound of it, he seemed to be a SK Hynix shareholder. Samsung Electronics got a mention as well.

Someone once said, “The market is always right.”

I take that to mean something a little simpler:

The market is just the market. That’s how it is.

I think people are much the same.

I watch Divorce Camp and I’m Solo every week, and it seems like almost every problem starts with one simple thing:

trying to understand the other person is where things go wrong.

The market is a bit like a living thing.

If the KOSPI suddenly crashes to 3,000 or even 4,000 tomorrow, I reckon you just have to accept it.

And if it goes the other way and shoots up to 10,000 or 12,000, well, that’s the market too.

You might be surprised, sure. 😲

Like what happened to the KOSPI on 31 July.

But what do you actually gain by constantly complaining that you’ve been screwed over by institutions and foreign investors, or blaming leveraged ETFs and saying the whole thing is a rigged playing field?

If that’s genuinely how you see the market, then maybe the sensible thing is simply not to play a game you believe is unfair in the first place.
