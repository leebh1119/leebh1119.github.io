It’s been about four or five months since I came back from Australia.

Since last week, the urge to study abroad has started creeping back in again.

One of the biggest reasons I decided to study in Australia in the first place was the feeling that staying at home like this was simply a waste of time.

On top of that, trying to prepare for an overseas job from Korea sometimes feels like banging my head against a wall. To become a professional quant, I also need to improve my English, and quite a few of the job postings have been for positions requiring a master's degree.

I had always been thinking about going abroad, whether for a degree or just to study English, but this time I started properly looking into universities and courses.

The countries that have always been somewhere in the back of my mind are New Zealand, Canada, and the UK. One reason is that I can use the English test score I submitted when I applied to study in Australia. It seems to be recognised in most Commonwealth countries.

In Australia, I chose the Master of Property Development at the University of Technology Sydney (UTS) without giving it much thought. That experience made me realise how important choosing the right major is. I felt that I should study something I genuinely wanted to do, and I've been leaning towards Data Science ever since.

I was thinking less about immigration and more about the subject itself, the possibility of finding work locally, and whether it would be something I could actually make use of later on.

New Zealand was also an option I looked into when I was preparing to study in Australia. I received offers from the University of Auckland and Auckland University of Technology, but in the end I chose Australia. Sometimes I wonder what I would be doing now if I had gone to New Zealand instead.

One of the biggest reasons I gave up on Australia was that I couldn't apply for a graduate visa. I already knew that before I went, but knowing something and actually experiencing it myself felt completely different.

After weighing up various factors, including the weather and climate, I eventually started considering London, which is the option I am currently thinking about the most. I don't know whether I'll actually be able to find a job there in the future, but the fact that it has a well-developed quant industry makes it an appealing option.

The exchange rate and the brutal cost of living are definitely concerns.

I also have a bit of a romantic fascination with London itself. When I was younger, studying there was something I couldn't even imagine doing. I had a great time when I visited London in 2018.

I found one of the universities within the University of London particularly appealing. I spoke to an agency, and they said the university is in Central London, Zone 1, and that my English score and undergraduate grades should be fine. Speaking of rent, it seems slightly more expensive than Sydney. I paid around £1,500 a month for a one-bedroom apartment.

Still, apparently London doesn't have quite the same obsession with rental history as Sydney. I spent an exhausting month trying to find a place to rent in Sydney under my own name, so that part sounds like a relief.

The documents I need should also be fairly straightforward, since I already prepared most of them when I applied to study in Australia.

I'm still only at the thinking stage, but I don't want to build everything around a fantasy. I should make an actual plan and try to picture what everyday life in London would really be like.
